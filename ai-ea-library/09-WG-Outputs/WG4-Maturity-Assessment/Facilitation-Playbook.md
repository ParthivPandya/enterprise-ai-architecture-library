# AI Maturity Assessment Facilitation Playbook

| Metadata | Description |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Help a neutral facilitator run an inclusive, evidence-based AI maturity assessment and preserve disagreement and uncertainty. |
| **Audience** | Facilitators, enterprise architects, capability owners, risk and compliance functions, internal assurance teams and organisational sponsors. |
| **Use When** | Conducting a cross-functional assessment workshop or reconciling independently completed responses. |
| **Outputs** | Agreed scope, evidence register, scored instrument, dissent log, heatmap inputs and action questions. |
| **Content classification** | Facilitation safeguards and decision rules are **Normative** for adopters. Suggested questions and techniques are **Informative**. |

## 1. Facilitation principles — Normative

The facilitator shall:

- remain neutral about the desired score;
- require evidence before accepting a score;
- prevent seniority from substituting for evidence;
- give control, operational and affected-user perspectives space to challenge;
- keep “Not assessed” and dissent visible;
- separate capability diagnosis from individual performance;
- protect confidential evidence; and
- avoid presenting the exercise as certification, ranking or assurance.

## 2. Roles — Normative

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Sponsor | States the organisational decision and ensures access to relevant roles and evidence |
| Facilitator | Applies the method, manages discussion and records rationale without directing the result |
| Evidence custodian | Provides controlled references and confirms source authority |
| Respondents | Score independently, cite evidence and disclose uncertainty |
| Domain reviewers | Challenge statements requiring specialist knowledge |
| Recorder | Maintains scores, evidence references, dissent and open questions |

One person may hold more than one role in a small organisation, but the sponsor should not unilaterally set scores.

## 3. Preparation workflow — Normative

1. **Define the decision:** State why the assessment is needed and what it will not decide.
2. **Set scope:** Identify organisational units, AI uses, platforms and evidence boundaries.
3. **Select participants:** Include strategy, business, data, architecture, engineering, operations, security, privacy, risk and finance perspectives as relevant.
4. **Distribute the instrument:** Ask respondents to score independently and cite evidence before discussion.
5. **Build an evidence register:** Record owner, source, scope, access and limitation.
6. **Identify sensitive material:** Decide how it will be referenced without broad disclosure.
7. **Configure the scorecard:** Prepare statement rows, confidence fields and dissent capture.

## 4. Session sequence — Normative

### A. Opening

Explain the decision, scope, scoring scale, evidence rule and confidentiality boundary. Confirm that the goal is an honest capability profile, not the highest score.

### B. Independent positions

Capture each respondent’s proposed score before group discussion. This reduces conformity pressure and makes genuine differences visible.

### C. Evidence review

For each statement, ask:

- What observable practice supports this score?
- Does the evidence cover the whole agreed scope?
- Is it a documented intention or repeated operation?
- What evidence contradicts it?
- Who is missing from this interpretation?

### D. Reconciliation

Adopt the highest score for which every defining characteristic is supported. If evidence supports different scores in different units, record separate profiles or the lower scope-wide score. Keep dissent when participants cannot resolve a material interpretation.

### E. Constraint discussion

Identify statements essential to the decision. Discuss consequences and dependencies, not blame. Do not choose actions solely because a colour appears weak.

### F. Close

Read back agreed scores, unknowns, dissent, evidence gaps and decision constraints. Confirm who may correct factual recording errors. Do not convert unresolved judgement into apparent consensus.

## 5. Facilitation techniques — Informative

| Challenge | Technique |
|---|---|
| Senior participant anchors the discussion | Collect scores silently first; request evidence before debate |
| Policy is presented as proof | Ask for a sampled case showing the policy in operation |
| One successful team dominates | Restate the scope and request evidence from other units |
| Participants debate terminology | Return to the observable characteristics in the scoring scale |
| Discussion becomes personal | Reframe around system capability, evidence and consequence |
| Evidence is confidential | Use an authorised reviewer to attest to defined properties without sharing content |
| Unknowns are uncomfortable | Record “Not assessed” and specify the evidence needed |
| Scores are negotiated | Reapply the strongest-level-fully-supported rule |

| Statement | Views expressed | Evidence difference | Agreed treatment | Decision impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | |

Material dissent should accompany the scorecard. A majority vote is unsuitable when disagreement concerns evidence authority, safety, privacy, law or formal accountability.

## 8. Question bank — Informative

- Show a recent example where this capability changed a decision.
- What happens when the defined practice is not followed?
- How is the responsible person made aware?
- Which part depends on individual memory or goodwill?
- How do you know the control works in operation?
- What evidence would cause you to lower the score?
- Does the practice work for externally supplied AI?
- How are incidents or user challenges reflected in architecture?
- Could a different business unit give the same evidence?

## 9. Completion checks — Normative

- [ ] The decision and scope remain unchanged or any change is recorded.
- [ ] Relevant perspectives participated.
- [ ] Independent scores were captured before reconciliation.
- [ ] Every agreed score cites evidence.
- [ ] Confidence, unknowns and dissent are visible.
- [ ] Sensitive evidence remains access-controlled.
- [ ] The heatmap preserves minimum scores and essential gaps.
- [ ] The action discussion focuses on causes and dependencies.
- [ ] No score is described as certification, assurance or guaranteed readiness.
