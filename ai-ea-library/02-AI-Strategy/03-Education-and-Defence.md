## PART ONE: AI in Education

### The State of AI in Education (2025–2026)

Education is one of the fastest-changing sectors for AI adoption. The numbers are striking:

- Global student AI usage jumped from 66% to 92% between 2024 and 2025 — the steepest year-over-year increase on record (HEPI Student Generative AI Survey)
- K-12 teacher AI adoption doubled from 25% to 53% in a single school year (2023–24 to 2024–25)
- Institution-wide AI adoption in higher education surged from 49% to 66% in 2025 — a 17-point jump signalling the move from experimentation to mainstream operational integration (Ellucian Survey 2026)
- Yet only 10% of schools and universities worldwide have formal AI guidelines (UNESCO)

The central challenge: **adoption has dramatically outpaced governance**.

### AI Use Cases in K-12 Education

#### Administrative Automation
The highest-ROI AI use cases in K-12 are administrative, not instructional:

| Use Case | What AI Does | Measured Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| IEP (Individualised Education Plan) preparation | Drafts IEP goals from student assessment data | **90% reduction in preparation time** (RaiseSummit 2026) |
| Lesson plan generation | Generates differentiated lesson plans from learning objectives and student data | Teachers save 5.9 hrs/week (weekly users, Gallup-Walton 2025) |
| Parent communication | Drafts communications, translates to home languages | Reduced admin burden; improved family engagement |
| Assessment grading | Marks written work with rubric-based feedback | Faster feedback cycles |
| Timetabling optimisation | Optimises class schedules from room, teacher, and curriculum constraints | Hours of planning to minutes |

**Case Study — Special Education AI (Washington State, 2026):**
Rose Prust, a Special Education Teacher at Lopez Island School District, used Magic School and Ablespace to refine IEP goals for students with complex needs. Beyond paperwork efficiency: by analysing interaction data through Ablespace, the AI discovered that a student with cortical visual impairment responded better to auditory cues than visual ones — an insight that changed the instructional approach. By the 2024–25 school year, 57% of special education teachers were using AI to assist with IEPs or 504 plans, up from 39% the previous year.

**Quality insight:** Teachers using AI to draft IEP goals scored 9.1–10.0 on a 10-point quality scale, compared to 5.5–9.2 for those working without AI. Governance implication: AI-assisted IEPs require a quality framework and human review, not just a tool deployment.

#### Personalised Learning

Adaptive learning platforms use AI to:
- Diagnose knowledge gaps at the student level
- Serve practice problems calibrated to each student's current performance
- Adjust pacing and difficulty in real time
- Alert teachers to students falling behind

**Outcomes from controlled studies:**
- AI tutors have helped students learn **twice as fast** as peers in some reading and mathematics interventions
- The Khan Academy Khanmigo deployment showed meaningful improvement in student engagement and comprehension

**The governance challenge:** Personalised learning AI requires access to detailed student data (performance, behaviour, sometimes biometric) — data that triggers significant privacy obligations (FERPA in the US, DPDPA in India, GDPR in Europe).

#### Classroom AI (Teacher Tools)
- **Content generation:** Worksheet, quiz, and rubric creation from curriculum standards
- **Differentiation support:** Generating materials at multiple reading levels simultaneously
- **Feedback augmentation:** AI-generated formative feedback at scale that human teachers review and personalise

### AI Use Cases in Higher Education

**Case Study — Arizona State University (ASU) AI Innovation Challenge (2024):**
Launched February 2024, the initiative attracted over 175 proposals within two weeks. By July 2024, more than 200 projects were underway across 80% of ASU's academic units. One standout: the "Sam" chatbot in the College of Health Solutions, which allowed students to practise patient-provider interactions — offering realistic scenarios to build motivational interviewing skills and delivering qualitative feedback that previously required dedicated simulation labs.

**Case Study — Carnegie Mellon University AI tutoring:**
CMU's use of AI-powered tutoring in introductory statistics courses showed measurably improved exam performance compared to control groups without AI tutoring access. The key design element: AI tutored problem-solving, not answer provision.

| Function | What AI Does | Adoption Status |
|---|---|---|
| Student retention prediction | Flags at-risk students from engagement, grade, and attendance signals | 43% of institutions include AI in strategic plans now (vs. lower baseline in 2024) |

### AI Governance Framework for Educational Institutions

The gap between AI adoption and AI governance in education is dangerous: 92% usage, 10% formal policies.

**Minimum viable governance for schools and universities:**

1. **AI Use Policy** — distinguishes between permitted, restricted, and prohibited uses by student/faculty/staff roles. Addresses academic integrity specifically.

2. **Data Privacy Review** — before any AI tool is deployed, evaluate data collected, storage location, third-party sharing, and applicable regulation (FERPA/DPDPA/GDPR).

3. **Vendor Assessment** — schools and universities process sensitive student data. AI vendors must meet the same security and compliance standards as any other data processor.

4. **Teacher/Faculty Competency** — 45% of educators lack AI training despite high tool adoption (Cengage Group 2025). Training reduces both misuse and underuse.

5. **Academic Integrity Framework** — defines the role of AI in coursework by assignment type; establishes detection and response processes.

6. **Student Communication** — students need to know when AI is being used to assess, monitor, or personalise their education.

## PART TWO: AI in Defence

### The Strategic Imperative

Defence organisations worldwide are in an AI arms race. The integration of AI into military systems is proceeding on three fronts simultaneously: **warfighting capability**, **enterprise management efficiency**, and **responsible AI governance**.

NATO Secretary General's position (2024 Washington Summit): AI adoption in defence is not optional — it is a deterrence requirement. Allied nations that lag in AI adoption will face military disadvantage within the decade.

### NATO's Revised AI Strategy (July 2024)

Endorsed at the 2024 Washington Summit, NATO's revised AI strategy has four primary aims:

1. **Responsible AI use for defence** — ethical principles, transparency, and human oversight
2. **Accelerated AI adoption** — interoperability between member state systems; speed to capability
3. **Safeguarding AI technologies** — protecting AI systems from adversarial exploitation and compromise
4. **Countering adversarial AI** — detecting and defending against AI-enabled information operations, autonomous weapons, and AI-assisted cyberattacks

**2025 Hague Summit:** Allied leaders endorsed NATO's Rapid Adoption Action Plan — an explicit programme to accelerate the pace of technology adoption across the alliance, with AI as the primary focus.

### US Department of Defense AI Deployment

**Task Force Lima (2023–2024):** Established by Deputy Defense Secretary Kathleen Hicks to evaluate and recommend generative AI capabilities across DoD. Identified 15 warfighting and enterprise management use cases.

**AI Rapid Capabilities Cell (AI RCC, December 2024):** Launched to execute 15 priority AI use cases, partnering with the Defense Innovation Unit (DIU). Four initial "Frontier AI Pilots" applying generative AI to warfighting and enterprise management.

**DoD AI Use Case Categories:**

| Category | Specific Applications |
|---|---|
| **Command & Control** | Decision support tools, intelligence synthesis, situational awareness dashboards |
| **Logistics & Sustainment** | Predictive maintenance, supply chain optimisation, demand forecasting for military equipment |
| **Training & Simulation** | AI-generated synthetic environments for exercises; realistic adversarial actor simulation |
| **Cybersecurity** | Threat detection, anomaly analysis, vulnerability assessment |
| **Administrative** | Document drafting, report summarisation, translation of technical materials |
| **Intelligence Analysis** | Multi-source intelligence fusion, pattern recognition, imagery analysis |
| **Software Development** | Code generation and review for defence systems |

**Case Study — Predictive Maintenance (US Air Force):**
AI-driven predictive maintenance for C-17 transport aircraft predicts component failures before they occur, scheduling maintenance proactively. Early results showed 20–30% reduction in unplanned maintenance events and improved aircraft availability rates.

**Case Study — Project Maven:**
The US DoD's Project Maven applies computer vision AI to drone surveillance imagery, automating object detection and classification that previously required large numbers of human analysts. Extended beyond imagery to synthetic aperture radar and multi-sensor fusion.

### Defence Industry AI Deployment

**Lockheed Martin:** AI integrated into F-35 logistics and maintenance systems. Predictive analytics reduce parts ordering lead time and improve mission-readiness.

**General Dynamics:** AI in armoured vehicle autonomy, cybersecurity platforms, and decision-support systems for command centres.

**Airbus Defence and Space:** AI in satellite systems, drone coordination, and cybersecurity platforms — improving reaction time and reducing operator cognitive load.

**Leonardo (Europe):** AI in air traffic management and surveillance, collaborating with NATO on interoperability standards.

### AI Governance in Defence: The Responsible AI Challenge

Defence AI governance is arguably the most demanding in any sector:

**Challenge 1: Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems (LAWS)**
The fundamental ethical and legal question: what level of human control is required for AI systems that can select and engage targets? Current US DoD policy: human judgment required for lethal force decisions. Policy subject to ongoing international debate.

**Challenge 2: Algorithmic Warfare Ethics**
AI systems used for surveillance, targeting, and intelligence analysis can make errors with fatal consequences. Bias in training data (e.g., models trained on one conflict zone applied in another) creates differential performance that must be assessed before deployment.

**Challenge 3: Adversarial AI**
NATO strategy explicitly addresses adversarial AI — the use of AI by adversaries for disinformation campaigns, deepfakes, autonomous cyber-attacks, and swarm drone coordination. Defence AI systems must be tested against AI-enabled adversarial attacks, not just conventional attacks.

**Challenge 4: Interoperability**
NATO members use AI from different vendors, trained on different data, with different capability profiles. Interoperability — the ability for allied AI systems to share, interpret, and trust each other's outputs — is a significant unresolved technical and governance challenge.

### The Responsible AI Principles for Defence (NATO & DoD)

Both NATO and the US DoD have published responsible AI principles for defence:

| Principle | US DoD | NATO |
|---|---|---|
| **Responsibility** | Human accountable for every AI-enabled decision | Human control and accountability |
| **Equitable** | Free from algorithmic bias; consistent performance across groups | Fair and impartial treatment |
| **Traceable** | Explainable, auditable, transparent | Transparent and explainable |
| **Reliable** | Tested across relevant scenarios; acceptable performance across context | Reliable and robust |
| **Governable** | Designed to allow disengagement and correction | Can be disengaged; failure modes known |

### Lessons for Enterprise Architects in Defence Contexts

1. **Dual-use awareness** — AI systems designed for one purpose often have security implications when repurposed. Architect for controlled use.

2. **Classification handling** — AI systems must respect data classification levels; RAG systems need access controls aligned to clearance levels.

3. **Offline/degraded operation** — battlefield and austere environment deployments require AI that functions without cloud connectivity. Edge deployment architecture is a first-class requirement.

4. **Human-machine teaming design** — the enterprise architecture question in defence is not "what should AI decide?" but "what is the right human-machine teaming model for each decision type?" Design the human interface as carefully as the AI capability.

5. **Procurement reform** — traditional defence procurement cycles (multi-year) are incompatible with AI model lifecycles (months). Enterprise architects must design for continuous model updates within procurement frameworks that weren't built for it.
